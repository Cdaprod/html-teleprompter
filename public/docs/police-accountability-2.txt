## How to Fish (What to Capture on Record)

**Objective:** Establish a clear, chronological record that the detention is unlawful **in the officer’s own words**.

1. **Establish no underlying crime**
   - Have the officer explicitly state that no crime has occurred  
   - Or that the alleged violation has already ended

2. **Confirm the detention**
   - Ask whether you are being detained or are free to leave  
   - Capture refusal to let you leave without citing a crime

3. **Identity seizure attempt**
   - Have the officer request ID  
   - Refuse politely and ask for the legal basis  
   - Capture that no crime or reasonable suspicion is articulated

4. **Threat escalation**
   - Capture an explicit or implied threat of arrest  
   - Especially when no crime has been stated

5. **Admission of pretext**
   - Have the officer say:
     - "There’s no crime"
     - "It’s just an investigation"
     - "I just want to check for warrants"
     - or similar justification detached from an offense

6. **Reason drift**
   - Allow the officer to cycle through:
     - vague safety concerns  
     - demeanor-based suspicion  
     - authority-based statements  
   - Document the inconsistency and shifting rationale

7. **Articulate your position (calmly)**
   - State, on record:
     - You do not consent to detention or searches  
     - You are asserting your right to remain silent  
     - You understand ID is not required absent a crime  
     - You are not obstructing and are willing to leave

8. **Let the record speak**
   - Stop filling silence  
   - Allow the contradiction to exist without argument  
   - The value is in *what they say*, not what you prove in the moment

Call 1: The Sheriff's Office/Public Information Officer

Goal: Get an on-the-record response about the policy and justification for "Aggressive Criminal Enforcement" and pretextual stops.

Pre-Call Setup:

· Record the call (Check Georgia consent law! Georgia is a one-party consent state. You can legally record if you are a participant. Announce at the start: "For accuracy, this call is being recorded.").

· Have the Georgia Code (40-6-14) and the legal standards (Terry v. Ohio, reasonable suspicion) in front of you.

Potential Script/Questions:

"Hi, I'm calling regarding an incident on [Date] involving one of your deputies from a unit marked 'Aggressive Criminal Enforcement.' I'm making a documentary about policing and I'd like to ask a few questions for clarity.

1. Policy Question: Can you explain the department's official policy behind the 'Aggressive Criminal Enforcement' slogan on patrol vehicles? What does 'aggressive' mean in this context, and how does the policy ensure it doesn't lead to violations of constitutional rights against unreasonable seizures?
2. Training Question: How are deputies trained on the legal standard for a noise violation under GA Code 40-6-14?
